01 / Overview

Project Overview

Located in Buckland, Portsmouth, this project explores how architecture can help improve food distribution and create a stronger sense of community. Based on the 18th Sustainable Development Goal, Even Food Distribution, the aim is to make fresh and nutritious food more accessible to everyone while encouraging more sustainable ways of growing, sharing and distributing food.

The building combines residential and educational spaces, bringing together 20 apartments, research laboratories, classrooms and community facilities. The apartments range from 35 m² to 139 m² and accommodate between one and four people, including displaced families, local workers and researchers. Each unit has access to private or shared kitchens, with balconies and communal gardens providing additional spaces for growing and socialising.

Food production is a key part of the project, combining indoor hydroponic growing with traditional outdoor planting. The building creates opportunities for residents and the wider community to get involved in growing food, learning about nutrition and understanding more sustainable farming methods. Produce grown on-site can be shared with residents or sold locally, helping to make fresh food more affordable and accessible while reducing food waste.

The design started with an analysis of Seymour Close, which was identified as a disconnected and cramped residential area. To open up the site and improve accessibility, around 31 houses and part of the existing street were removed. This allowed for better pedestrian and cycle routes, while the existing car park was transformed into a productive landscape, with fruit trees replacing the original planting.

The building's form developed through experimenting with softer, curved shapes inspired by the boundaries of the site. The floor plates gradually step back as the building rises towards a rooftop greenhouse, creating balconies and terraces that provide space for planting, circulation and social interaction.

Circulation also played an important role in shaping the design. Rather than creating a traditional central atrium, I wanted the building to feel more open and connected to its surroundings. A public walkway runs through the building, with openings at ground level and bridges connecting different areas.

Overall, the project aims to bring housing, food production and education together in one place, creating a building that is not only used by the community but also contributes to it.
